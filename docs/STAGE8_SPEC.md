## 1. Why this is the right next stage

Two reasons, and the second is the real one.

**The compute is already being thrown away.** `config.PLANCK_2018_BASE` sets
`"output": "tCl,pCl,lCl"`, so CLASS is already computing polarisation on every
single call. `spectra.run_class` then takes `cls["tt"]` and discards
`cls["ee"]` and `cls["te"]`. Every sweep already run has silently computed the
EE spectrum and dropped it. Adding polarisation costs no extra CLASS time.

**EE is a discriminator for the Stage 5 open question.** That is the point.
Not "we also plotted EE."

Stage 5 established that across the ω_cdm grid, TT peaks 1, 2 and 3 lose
48.4%, 45.2% and 16.5% of their height. Peak 3 resists. Four mechanisms were
eliminated and the cause is still unknown.

The question Stage 8 can actually answer is narrower and answerable: **is that
resistance a property of how modes at that scale respond, or something
specific to the density oscillation that TT measures?**

EE separates those because it is sourced by a different field.

---

## 2. The physics, in enough detail to defend it

**TT peaks come from the density oscillation.** The observed temperature
anisotropy at last scattering tracks the effective temperature of the
photon-baryon fluid, the monopole plus the potential. Baryon loading shifts
the *zero point* of that oscillation deeper into the potential well, so
compressions overshoot rarefactions. That asymmetry is the odd/even peak
modulation, and it is the whole content of your Stage 6 ω_b sweep.

**EE comes from the velocity field.** Polarisation is generated by Thomson
scattering off a local quadrupole, and that quadrupole is produced by the
velocity gradient of the fluid at last scattering. The velocity oscillates
about zero. Baryon loading does not displace its zero point the way it
displaces the density oscillation's.

**The consequence, stated carefully.** EE lacks the *alternating* odd/even
modulation that TT has. That is not the same as "EE is independent of
baryons." Baryons still enter EE through the sound speed (so through peak
positions), through the damping scale, and through the overall envelope. The
specific thing EE is missing is the zero-point displacement that makes odd TT
peaks tall and even TT peaks short.

**The phase relationship, and it is visible in the real data.** Velocity
extrema fall a quarter wavelength from density extrema, so EE peaks should
interleave with TT peaks. Planck measures TT peaks at ℓ = 220.6, 538.1, 809.8.
In `COM_PowerSpect_CMB-EE-binned_R3.02.txt` the bandpowers reach local maxima
in the bins centred near ℓ ≈ 135, 405, 705, 1005 and 1305. Those sit between
the TT peaks. You do not have to take the phase argument on faith; the two
files show it.

## 3. The diagnostic, and what each outcome would mean

**Write this down before running anything.** This is the Stage 5 lesson
applied properly: two badly designed tests in Stage 5 could not distinguish
the hypotheses they were aimed at, and both made good results look like
failures. The fix is to state the implication map first.

Run the ω_cdm sweep at fixed θ_s, extract EE peak heights, and compute the
same "which peak resists" measurement that gave 48.4% / 45.2% / 16.5% in TT.

| If the EE result is | Then |
|---|---|
| EE also shows one peak resisting far more than its neighbours | The effect lives in how modes at that scale respond to driving, and is common to both observables. Anything requiring the density-oscillation zero point is ruled out. |
| EE peaks fall more uniformly than TT peaks do | The resistance is specific to the density oscillation, which points at the interplay between radiation driving and baryon loading in TT. |
| EE is too noisy or the peak finder is unstable | Record that and stop. Do not tune the finder until a signal appears. |

**No predicted sign goes in this table on purpose.** Stage 2 predicted three
signs, got two wrong, and correct results looked like bugs. Record which row
you land in. Do not debug toward one.

Note what this test does *not* do: it does not propose a fifth mechanism, and
it will not by itself identify the cause. It narrows where the cause can live.
That is a legitimate result and should be written up as one.

## 4. Scope decision: EE carries the analysis, TE does not

**TE changes sign, so peak-height ratios are meaningless for it.** In
`COM_PowerSpect_CMB-TE-binned_R3.02.txt` the bandpowers run from about
−127 µK² to +119 µK², crossing zero repeatedly. A ratio of two numbers that
straddle zero is not an observable, it is a discontinuity waiting to happen.

So:

- **EE** gets the peak finding, the sweep, and the ratio analysis.
- **TE** gets an overlay against the baseline and a diagonal χ² only. It is a
  pipeline validation, not a peak-height result.

Say this explicitly in the writeup. A reader who knows CMB analysis will
wonder why TE was left out, and "because D_ℓ^TE crosses zero" is the right
answer.

## 5. The peak-numbering decision you must make explicitly

**This is the most likely way to get a wrong answer that looks right.**

TT peak *n* and EE peak *n* are not the same physical mode. The EE bandpowers
show a weak feature near ℓ ≈ 135 and strong maxima near 405, 705, 1005. If the
ℓ ≈ 135 feature counts as EE peak 1, then EE peak 3 is near ℓ ≈ 705 and sits
between TT peaks 2 and 3. If it does not count, everything shifts by one.

Comparing "TT peak 3" to "EE peak 3" without settling this compares two
different scales and the result means nothing.

**Decide and document the mapping before running the sweep.** The defensible
choice is to compare at matched multipole rather than matched index: for each
TT peak, find where the EE spectrum sits at a comparable ℓ, or compare the
full EE and TT peak sequences as functions of ℓ rather than pairing them by
number. Whichever you pick, write one paragraph justifying it, because this is
exactly the kind of choice a professor will ask about.

**Decided (2026-07-27).** Two decisions, settled after step 5's peak-finder
work:

1. **The ℓ ≈ 140 feature is a real EE acoustic feature, not a finder
   artifact** — it is a genuine local maximum of the theory curve (D_ℓ ≈ 1.12
   µK², scipy prominence ≈ 0.4345 µK²), not noise or a bug. It is excluded
   from the sweep's default preset (`EE_PEAK_PROMINENCE = 5.0` in config.py)
   for a stability reason, not a physical one: its prominence is close enough
   to zero that it is expected to disappear at some points on the ω_cdm grid,
   which would give an inconsistent peak count and NaN rows partway through a
   sweep that otherwise runs clean. The five strong maxima (prominence 9.4-34.6
   µK², a ~20x gap above the weak feature) are stable across the grid instead.
   Excluded for sweep stability, not because it isn't real.

2. **TT and EE peaks are never paired by index.** TT peak 3 sits at ℓ = 813;
   EE's strong maxima straddle it, at ℓ = 688 and ℓ = 990. Pairing "TT peak 3"
   with "EE peak 3" would compare two different physical scales, exactly the
   trap this section warns about. The comparison instead plots fractional
   peak-height change against ℓ_peak for both spectra on the same axes — no
   peak index is compared across spectra, only position and normalised height.
   This also keeps envelope shape from leaking into the comparison: on the
   baseline curve, TT peak height falls monotonically from peak 1
   (1.00 / 0.45 / 0.44 normalised to peak 1), while EE's five strong maxima
   rise to a maximum at ℓ ≈ 990 and then fall (0.51 / 0.89 / 1.00 / 0.72 / 0.49
   normalised to the tallest). Pairing by index would let that shape
   difference masquerade as a resistance signal.

## 6. Traps, in order of how much they will cost you

**1. The Planck R3.01 TE/EE files have their contents swapped.** This is real
and documented on the Planck Legacy Archive wiki:
`COM_PowerSpect_CMB-TE-binned_R3.01.txt` actually contains EE, and
`COM_PowerSpect_CMB-EE-binned_R3.01.txt` actually contains TE. They were
replaced by **R3.02** for exactly this reason.

If you grab R3.01 out of habit because the TT file you already have is R3.01,
every EE conclusion in Stage 8 will silently be a TE conclusion. Nothing will
error. Use R3.02 for both, and add an assertion that the EE file's values are
positive across the acoustic range, since TE is not.

**2. `find_acoustic_peaks(prominence=50.0)` will find zero EE peaks.** TT peak
heights are thousands of µK²; EE bandpowers max out around 21 to 42 µK². The
default prominence is larger than the entire EE signal. This will present as a
`ValueError: found 0 peaks`, which is at least a loud failure rather than a
silent one.

**3. `first_peak_bounds=(180.0, 280.0)` is TT-specific** and will reject a
correctly-found EE spectrum.

**4. EE and TE have fewer bins than TT.** The TT file has 83 bins running to
ℓ_eff = 2499.02. The EE and TE files stop at ℓ_eff = 1988.02 with roughly 66
bins. Assert the shape, do not assume the arrays line up.

**5. But the bin centres are identical where they overlap.** Both start at
ℓ_eff = 47.7112240 and step identically. That means `bin_theory_to_planck` and
the whole Stage 7 χ² machinery transfer unchanged. Good news, worth checking
rather than assuming.

- `PLANCK_EE_BINNED`, `PLANCK_TE_BINNED` paths and their R3.02 URLs on the
  IRSA mirror, alongside the existing TT pair.
- A comment recording the R3.01 swap, so nobody "helpfully" downgrades.

  call site, matching how the rest of the config is organised.

### `src/cmbpeaks/spectra.py`

  return, defaulting to TT so every existing caller is untouched.
- No change to the CLASS `output` string. `pCl` is already there.
- `cl_to_dl` needs no change. The ℓ(ℓ+1)/2π and T_cmb² conversion is the same

### `src/cmbpeaks/peaks.py`

- `find_acoustic_peaks` needs EE-appropriate `prominence`, `first_peak_bounds`
  and possibly `ell_max_search`. Pass them explicitly or add a named preset.
  **Do not change the defaults** — that would move Stage 1–7 results.

### `src/cmbpeaks/planck.py`

- `load_planck_tt` already matches the EE/TE column layout exactly
  (`l  Dl  -dDl  +dDl  BestFit`). Generalise it to take a path, keep a thin

- `PlanckTT` dataclass either gets a `spectrum` field or a more honest name.

### `src/cmbpeaks/sweep.py`

- `SweepResult` records one spectrum's peaks. Add a `spectrum` field and save

- Follow the Stage 6 precedent in `SweepResult.load`: older files lack the new
  key, so default it rather than raising. That pattern already exists for
  `param_values` and should be copied, not reinvented.

### `src/cmbpeaks/plotting.py`

- Baseline EE overlay against Planck EE, mirroring `plot_baseline_overlay`.
- TE overlay, same idea, with a zero line since TE crosses it.
- An EE version of `plot_peak_heights_normalised`, which is the figure that
  actually answers section 3.

### `scripts/`

- `10_baseline_polarisation.py` — baseline EE and TE overlays, benchmark print.
- `11_sweep_ee.py` — the ω_cdm EE sweep and the section 3 figure.

### `tests/`

## 9. What Stage 8 must not claim

- Not a measurement of anything.
- Not a detection. The EE result narrows where the Stage 5 cause can live; it
  does not identify it.
- Not novel. Polarisation peak structure has been measured since DASI in 2002
  and modelled since the 1990s.
- The TE χ² inherits every caveat from Stage 7, plus the extra wrinkle that a
  spectrum crossing zero makes a fractional residual meaningless near the
  crossings.
